\begin{abstract}
This paper studies behind-the-meter battery planning for a grid-connected
data center supplied by the utility grid, co-located photovoltaic generation,
and modular diesel backup. The planning model sizes battery power and energy
under representative 48-h scenarios while accounting for grid outages,
time-varying electricity prices, and time-varying grid carbon intensity. A
linear carbon-vintage formulation associates stored energy with its physical
source and charging interval; consequently, battery discharge retains the
carbon intensity of the energy used to charge it. Carbon compliance is imposed
as an energy-weighted horizon budget with different allowances during normal
grid-connected operation and emergency islanded operation. Excess emissions
are feasible but explicitly priced, whereas involuntary load shedding is
restricted to outage intervals. To construct a small decision-relevant
scenario set, a conditional variational autoencoder is trained jointly with a
recourse-aware decision loss. Exact mixed-integer planning and fixed-design
recourse solutions provide detached severity signals, while gradients flow
through directional load, photovoltaic, and carbon forecast errors. An
aggregate source-emissions formulation is included as a controlled baseline so
that the value of carbon-vintage accounting can be isolated without changing
the physical system, scenarios, or training protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{ata-center} electricity demand is large, continuous, and
increasingly exposed to both reliability and carbon-performance requirements.
Behind-the-meter batteries can reduce peak purchases, exploit co-located PV,
and bridge the residual power deficit during utility outages. Their planning
value, however, depends on the joint occurrence of demand, PV availability,
grid carbon intensity, tariffs, and outages. A tractable planning study must
therefore address both temporal carbon accounting and scenario reduction.

A common aggregate-emissions calculation charges the battery when electricity
enters storage but treats later discharge without retaining the charging
source. That approximation can misstate the delivered carbon benefit of
shifting energy between hours. The formulation in this paper instead uses
virtual carbon vintages. Each vintage is linear, source identifiable, and
auditable; no bilinear state-of-carbon variable or carbon-flow relaxation is
required. This is particularly useful for a single point of common coupling
(PCC), where feeder power-flow and nodal-mixing models would add complexity
without representing the system implemented in the case study.

Scenario reduction creates a second difficulty. A statistically accurate
generator need not preserve rare combinations that change the capacity
decision. Decision-focused learning (DFL) addresses this mismatch by training
the scenario generator with feedback from planning and recourse problems
\cite{Donti2017TaskBased}. Here, the mixed-integer solver remains a detached
black box. Differentiable directional losses use its physical violation
magnitudes to correct optimistic or overly conservative generated scenarios.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item An exact linear battery carbon-vintage model is developed for a
grid-connected data-center PCC with PV, diesel backup, and outage-only load
shedding.
\item A mixed normal/outage horizon carbon budget separates routine carbon
compliance from emergency reliability while retaining all emissions in an
auditable ledger.
\item A joint CVAE--DFL procedure combines reconstruction with
infeasibility- and optimality-preserving recourse losses, including a
grid-availability mask for carbon learning.
\item A source-average carbon formulation is defined as a controlled baseline;
all noncarbon physics, costs, scenarios, and training settings are held fixed.
\end{itemize}

\section{System and Planning Problem}
\label{sec:planning}

\subsection{Scenario Representation}
The implemented system has one PCC. Scenario $s$ is a 48-h trajectory with
hourly resolution and contains $d_{s,t}$, $r_{s,t}$, $a_{s,t}$,
$\lambda_{s,t}$, and $\gamma_{s,t}$. The facility demand is constructed from a
workload/PUE proxy but is inelastic in the planning model; workload shifting
and deferrable-computing variables are not included. For probability
$\pi_s$, the annualization factor is
\begin{equation}
A_s=\pi_s\frac{8760}{|\mathcal T|\Delta t}.
\label{eq:annualization}
\end{equation}
All first-stage decisions are shared by the support scenarios, whereas
dispatch variables are scenario dependent.

\subsection{Battery Investment and Objective}
The first stage selects installation $z\in\{0,1\}$, power $P$, and energy $E$:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:sizing}
\begin{align}
\underline Pz\le P\le\overline Pz,\qquad
\underline Ez\le E\le\overline Ez,\label{eq:sizing_bounds}\\
\underline hP\le E\le\overline hP.\label{eq:duration}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The annual investment cost is
\begin{equation}
C^{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{CRF}\,\beta^{\mathrm{cap}}
\left(c^zz+c^PP+c^EE\right),
\label{eq:investment}
\end{equation}
where $\beta^{\mathrm{cap}}$ is a sensitivity multiplier. The complete
two-stage objective is
\begin{align}
\min\quad &C^{\mathrm{inv}}+c^{\mathrm{pk}}p^{\mathrm{pk}}
 +\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}A_s\Bigg\{
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\Delta t\bigg[
 \lambda_{s,t}p^{\mathrm g}_{s,t}
 +c^{\mathrm{dg}}p^{\mathrm{dg}}_{s,t}\nonumber\\
&\quad+c^{\mathrm{deg}}(p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t})
 +c^{\mathrm{curt}}p^{\mathrm{curt}}_{s,t}
 +c^{\mathrm{shed}}u_{s,t}\bigg]
 +c^{\mathrm{CO_2}}\Xi_s\Bigg\}.
\label{eq:objective}
\end{align}
Thus, carbon compliance is soft but priced. It becomes a hard constraint only
when $\Xi_s$ is removed.

\subsection{PCC Dispatch and Reliability}
Let $q\in\{\mathrm g,\mathrm{pv},\mathrm{dg}\}$ identify grid, PV, and diesel
sources. Source power is split between direct load supply and battery charging:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:source_totals}
\begin{align}
p^{\mathrm g}_{s,t}&=p^{\mathrm{g,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{g,ch}}_{s,t},\\
p^{\mathrm{dg}}_{s,t}&=p^{\mathrm{dg,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{dg,ch}}_{s,t},\\
p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t}&=p^{\mathrm{g,ch}}_{s,t}
+p^{\mathrm{pv,ch}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{dg,ch}}_{s,t},\\
p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t}&=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_s}
p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,\ell,t}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The load and PV balances are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:pcc_balance}
\begin{align}
p^{\mathrm{g,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{pv,l}}_{s,t}
+p^{\mathrm{dg,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t}
&=d_{s,t}-u_{s,t},\label{eq:load_balance}\\
p^{\mathrm{pv,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{pv,ch}}_{s,t}
+p^{\mathrm{curt}}_{s,t}&=r_{s,t}.\label{eq:pv_balance}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Grid availability, peak demand, and aggregate diesel capacity satisfy
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:source_limits}
\begin{align}
0\le p^{\mathrm g}_{s,t}&\le a_{s,t}\overline P^{\mathrm g},\qquad
p^{\mathrm g}_{s,t}\le p^{\mathrm{pk}},\\
0\le p^{\mathrm{dg}}_{s,t}&\le\overline P^{\mathrm{dg}},\label{eq:diesel_limit}\\
0\le u_{s,t}&\le d_{s,t}(1-a_{s,t}).\label{eq:outage_shed}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Equation~\eqref{eq:outage_shed} prohibits economic load shedding while the
grid is available. The case study uses three 2-MW diesel modules, represented
by $\overline P^{\mathrm{dg}}=6$ MW.

Binary variables $b^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t}$ and
$b^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t}$ prevent simultaneous charging and discharging:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:battery_power}
\begin{align}
0\le p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t}&\le P,quad
p^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t}\le\overline P b^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t},\\
0\le p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t}&\le P,quad
p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t}\le\overline P b^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t},\\
b^{\mathrm{ch}}_{s,t}+b^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,t}&\le z.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

\section{Carbon-Vintage Accounting}
\label{sec:carbon}

\subsection{Virtual Energy Layers}
For each scenario, define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_s=\{0,\mathrm{pv},\mathrm{dg}\}
\cup\{(\mathrm g,k):k\in\mathcal T\},
\label{eq:layers}
\end{equation}
where layer 0 is the initial inventory, the PV and diesel layers aggregate
fixed-intensity charging, and $(\mathrm g,k)$ is the grid vintage created at
hour $k$. Their fixed intensities are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:layer_intensity}
\begin{align}
\kappa_{s,0}&=\gamma^0,&
\kappa_{s,\mathrm{pv}}&=0,\\
\kappa_{s,\mathrm{dg}}&=\gamma^{\mathrm{dg}}/\eta^{\mathrm{ch}},&
\kappa_{s,(\mathrm g,k)}&=\gamma_{s,k}/\eta^{\mathrm{ch}}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Dividing by charging efficiency assigns the carbon of input electricity to
the smaller amount of energy retained in storage.

The layer dynamics are
\begin{align}
e_{s,\ell,t+1}={}&(1-\sigma)e_{s,\ell,t}
+\eta^{\mathrm{ch}}p^{\mathrm{add}}_{s,\ell,t}
-\frac{p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,\ell,t}}{\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}},
\label{eq:layer_balance}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
p^{\mathrm{add}}_{s,\ell,t}=\begin{cases}
p^{\mathrm{pv,ch}}_{s,t},&\ell=\mathrm{pv},\\
p^{\mathrm{dg,ch}}_{s,t},&\ell=\mathrm{dg},\\
p^{\mathrm{g,ch}}_{s,t},&\ell=(\mathrm g,t),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:layer_addition}
\end{equation}
Each layer is nonnegative and cannot discharge more than its available energy.
The aggregate state obeys
\begin{equation}
\underline{\mathrm{SOC}}E\le
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_s}e_{s,\ell,t}\le
\overline{\mathrm{SOC}}E.
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
Only the initial layer is populated at $t=0$:
\begin{equation}
e_{s,0,0}=\mathrm{SOC}^0E,\qquad e_{s,\ell,0}=0\quad(\ell\ne0).
\label{eq:initial_layers}
\end{equation}
The terminal conditions are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:terminal}
\begin{align}
\sum_{\ell}e_{s,\ell,T}&=\mathrm{SOC}^0E,\\
\sum_{\ell}\kappa_{s,\ell}e_{s,\ell,T}
&\le\gamma^0\mathrm{SOC}^0E.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The second condition prevents a scenario from satisfying its horizon budget by
ending with a dirtier battery than it started with.

\subsection{Delivered Carbon and Mixed Horizon Budget}
Under carbon-vintage accounting, delivered carbon rate is
\begin{align}
m^{\mathrm L}_{s,t}={}&\gamma_{s,t}p^{\mathrm{g,l}}_{s,t}
+\gamma^{\mathrm{dg}}p^{\mathrm{dg,l}}_{s,t}
+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L_s}\kappa_{s,\ell}
\frac{p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,\ell,t}}{\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}}.
\label{eq:layered_carbon}
\end{align}
The interval allowance is conditioned on grid availability:
\begin{equation}
\bar\gamma_{s,t}=\begin{cases}
\bar\gamma^{\mathrm g},&a_{s,t}=1,\\
\bar\gamma^{\mathrm o},&a_{s,t}=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:mixed_cap}
\end{equation}
Normal operation and emergency operation therefore enter one transparent
energy-weighted budget:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}m^{\mathrm L}_{s,t}\Delta t
\le\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\bar\gamma_{s,t}
(d_{s,t}-u_{s,t})\Delta t+\Xi_s,quad \Xi_s\ge0.
\label{eq:horizon_cap}
\end{equation}
The reported excess rate is $\Xi_s$ divided by served energy; it is not the
facility's absolute carbon intensity. The outage allowance is set to the diesel
intensity in the main case so that routine decarbonization does not obstruct
emergency supply.

For the temporal-resolution ablation, an hourly alternative replaces
\eqref{eq:horizon_cap} with
\begin{equation}
m^{\mathrm L}_{s,t}\le\bar\gamma_{s,t}(d_{s,t}-u_{s,t})+\Xi_{s,t}.
\label{eq:hourly_cap}
\end{equation}
The production experiment uses \eqref{eq:horizon_cap}.

\subsection{Aggregate Source-Average Baseline}
The controlled baseline retains every physical constraint above but replaces
\eqref{eq:layered_carbon} in the compliance budget by
\begin{align}
m^{\mathrm A}_{s,t}={}&\gamma_{s,t}
(p^{\mathrm{g,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{g,ch}}_{s,t})\nonumber\\
&+\gamma^{\mathrm{dg}}
(p^{\mathrm{dg,l}}_{s,t}+p^{\mathrm{dg,ch}}_{s,t}).
\label{eq:average_carbon}
\end{align}
Hence, source emissions are recognized when electricity enters the PCC or
battery, and the battery's charging-time source is not carried into the later
compliance calculation. The horizon-average intensity is
\begin{equation}
\bar m_s^{\mathrm A}=
\frac{\sum_t m^{\mathrm A}_{s,t}\Delta t}
{\sum_t(d_{s,t}-u_{s,t})\Delta t}.
\label{eq:average_intensity}
\end{equation}
This baseline is deliberately not given different costs, scenarios, terminal
conditions, or reliability limits. Comparing \eqref{eq:layered_carbon} and
\eqref{eq:average_carbon} therefore isolates the effect of carbon provenance.

\section{Decision-Focused Scenario Generation}
\label{sec:dfl}

\subsection{Conditional Generator and Joint Training}
Let $\xi$ collect the stochastic trajectories in one 48-h scenario and
$c$ denote its context. A CVAE parameterizes
\begin{equation}
z\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\qquad \widehat\xi=G_\phi(z,c),
\label{eq:cvae}
\end{equation}
and is trained with a reconstruction/KL loss augmented by field and shape
terms,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{stat}}=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}}+\beta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{KL}}
+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{shape}}.
\label{eq:stat_loss}
\end{equation}
The implemented joint experiment initializes $G_\phi$ randomly. Early epochs
use only \eqref{eq:stat_loss}; thereafter statistical and decision losses are
optimized together. Normalization parameters are fitted from the data and are
not learned generator weights.

For a generated support set $\widehat{\mathcal S}$, the planning oracle returns
\begin{equation}
\widehat x=x^*(\widehat{\mathcal S})
=\argmin_{x,y}\ C(x,y;\widehat{\mathcal S}).
\label{eq:generated_plan}
\end{equation}
The design $\widehat x$ is then fixed and redispatched on the corresponding
real scenarios. A perfect-information design $x^*(\mathcal S)$ is also solved
for the same training bag. The MILP solutions are detached; differentiation
does not pass through the argmin map.

\subsection{Directional Recourse Loss}
Let $\widehat d$, $\widehat r$, and $\widehat\gamma$ denote generated demand,
PV, and grid carbon. With scale $b_{s,t}=\max(d_{s,t},0.05)$, optimistic
directions are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:directions}
\begin{align}
\delta^d_{s,t}&=\frac{\pos{d_{s,t}-\widehat d_{s,t}}}{b_{s,t}},\\
\delta^r_{s,t}&=\frac{\pos{\widehat r_{s,t}-r_{s,t}}}{b_{s,t}}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
For carbon, define a smooth cap-relative pressure
\begin{equation}
\psi(\gamma)=\tau\log\left[1+
\exp\left(\frac{\gamma-\bar\gamma^{\mathrm g}}{\tau}\right)\right],
\quad \tau=\max(0.05,0.1\bar\gamma^{\mathrm g}).
\label{eq:carbon_pressure}
\end{equation}
Only grid-connected intervals contribute. Let $H_{s,t,k}$ be the detached
derivative of compliance carbon in delivery hour $t$ with respect to grid
intensity in source hour $k$. For layered accounting,
\begin{equation}
H_{s,t,k}=\mathbb I[k=t]p^{\mathrm{g,l}}_{s,t}
+\frac{p^{\mathrm{dis}}_{s,(\mathrm g,k),t}}
{\eta^{\mathrm{ch}}\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}};
\label{eq:vintage_exposure}
\end{equation}
for the average baseline it is
$H_{s,t,k}=\mathbb I[k=t]p^{\mathrm g}_{s,t}$.
The grid-carbon direction in compliance hour $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\delta^\gamma_{s,t}=\frac{1}{\bar\gamma^{\mathrm g}}
\pos{
\frac{\sum_k a_{s,t}a_{s,k}H_{s,t,k}
[\psi(\gamma_{s,k})-\psi(\widehat\gamma_{s,k})]}
{\sum_k a_{s,t}a_{s,k}H_{s,t,k}+\epsilon}}.
\label{eq:carbon_direction}
\end{equation}
This maps an error at a charging hour to the later hour in which its grid
vintage is actually discharged. The availability masks are essential:
grid-carbon forecasts have no physical role in an outage compliance interval,
for which the separate allowance $\bar\gamma^{\mathrm o}$ applies.

Let $\omega^u_{s,t}$ be normalized recourse shedding severity and
$\omega^\gamma_s$ be carbon-excess tons normalized by served energy. With
$h(v)=\operatorname{softplus}(m+v)-\operatorname{softplus}(m)$, the
infeasibility-penalty loss is
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{IPL}}={}&w_d\E[\omega^u h(\delta^d)]
+w_r\E[\omega^u h(\delta^r)]
+w_\gamma\E[\omega^\gamma h(\delta^\gamma)].
\label{eq:ipl}
\end{align}
The optimality-preserving loss fixes the perfect-information design and
redispatches it on generated scenarios. It uses the reverse directions
$\pos{\widehat d-d}$, $\pos{r-\widehat r}$, and the reverse of
\eqref{eq:carbon_direction}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{OPL}}=
w_d\E[\widetilde\omega^u h(\widetilde\delta^d)]
+w_r\E[\widetilde\omega^u h(\widetilde\delta^r)]
+w_\gamma\E[\widetilde\omega^\gamma h(\widetilde\delta^\gamma)].
\label{eq:opl}
\end{equation}
The decision loss and joint objective are
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:joint_loss}
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DFL}}&=\alpha\mathcal L_{\mathrm{IPL}}
+(1-\alpha)\mathcal L_{\mathrm{OPL}},\\
\mathcal L&=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{stat}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DFL}}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The implementation rescales $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$ so that the DFL decoder
gradient is a prescribed fraction of the statistical gradient, subject to
configured lower and upper bounds. This prevents either term from disappearing
solely because of units.

\subsection{Checkpoint Selection and Evaluation}
Validation holds the scenario bag, contexts, and latent supports fixed across
epochs. Because carbon performance is the target contribution, checkpoints are
ranked lexicographically by
\begin{equation}
\left(
\mathbb I[f^{\mathrm{shed}}>\varepsilon^{\mathrm{shed}}],
\pos{f^{\mathrm{shed}}-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{shed}}},
f^{\mathrm{CO_2}},
R
\right),
\label{eq:checkpoint_score}
\end{equation}
where $f^{\mathrm{shed}}$ is the fixed-validation shedding fraction,
$f^{\mathrm{CO_2}}$ is carbon excess per served MWh, and normalized decision
regret is
\begin{equation}
R=\frac{Q(\widehat x;\mathcal V)-Q(x^{\mathrm{PI}};\mathcal V)}
{|Q(x^{\mathrm{PI}};\mathcal V)|+\epsilon}.
\label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
Thus, a lower-carbon checkpoint is accepted only after satisfying the explicit
reliability guardrail. Final reporting uses an untouched test split and exact
fixed-design recourse. The no-storage value is
\begin{equation}
V^{\mathrm{ES}}=Q(0;\mathcal T^{\mathrm{test}})
-Q(\widehat x;\mathcal T^{\mathrm{test}}).
\label{eq:storage_value}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Joint CVAE--DFL Training}
\label{alg:training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Fit the scenario codec and initialize $G_\phi$ randomly.
\State Fix validation scenarios, support contexts, and latent vectors.
\For{$e=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{ep}}$}
  \State Update $G_\phi$ using $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{stat}}$.
  \If{$e$ is after the warm-up and is a DFL epoch}
    \State Solve planning on generated scenarios.
    \State Redispatch the design on real scenarios.
    \State Solve/lookup the perfect-information reference.
    \State Form \eqref{eq:ipl}--\eqref{eq:joint_loss} and update $G_\phi$.
  \EndIf
  \If{$e$ is a validation epoch}
    \State Update the incumbent checkpoint using \eqref{eq:checkpoint_score}.
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State Restore and save the best fixed-validation checkpoint.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper has formulated grid-connected data-center storage planning with an
exact linear carbon-vintage ledger and a mixed normal/outage carbon budget. The
joint CVAE--DFL method uses exact recourse severities without differentiating
through the mixed-integer solver, and its carbon loss is restricted to hours in
which grid-carbon forecasts are physically relevant. The controlled
source-average baseline enables the effect of carbon provenance to be measured
without changing system physics or training data. Quantitative conclusions
should be completed after both formulations have been trained and evaluated on
the common test set in Table~\ref{tab:carbon_ablation}.
