\section{Shared Storage Planning With Batch-Resolved Carbon Accounting}
\label{sec:model}

Consider a grid-connected microgrid represented by a fixed radial graph
$\mathscr{G}=(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{L})$, where bus $0$ is the point of
common coupling with the upstream grid. The microgrid contains
dispatchable generators, photovoltaic (PV) units, conventional loads, and
a data center at bus $i^{\mathrm{DC}}$. Shared energy storage can be
installed at candidate buses
$\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{es}}\subseteq\mathcal{N}$ and is centrally
dispatched to support the entire microgrid.

The operator determines the siting and rated power and energy capacities
of the shared storage and jointly schedules it with the other resources.
Planning decisions are common to all scenarios, whereas dispatch and
workload schedules are scenario dependent. The objective is to minimize
annualized cost subject to operating constraints and nodal
carbon-intensity targets.

The model departs from a well-mixed treatment of stored carbon. Each
charging interval creates a virtual energy batch, termed a
\emph{carbon vintage}, whose charging-time carbon intensity is preserved
until discharge. This retains the temporal heterogeneity of stored
electricity and, as Section~\ref{sec:value_origin} makes explicit, is what
allows the compliance value of an installation to depend on \emph{when}
it was charged rather than only on how much it holds.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Horizon and Scenario Structure}
\label{sec:horizon}

Each scenario $s\in\mathcal{S}$ is a 48-hour block,
$t\in\mathcal{T}=\{1,\dots,T\}$ with $T=48$ and $\Delta t=1$\,h, weighted
by
\begin{equation}
\rho_s=\frac{8760}{T\Delta t}
\label{eq:annualization}
\end{equation}
so that block operating costs annualize consistently.

Two days is a deliberate choice. It is long enough to contain a complete
charge--discharge cycle together with the overnight interval in which
stored low-carbon energy is most valuable, so the vintage mechanism is
exercised rather than truncated. It is also short enough to keep a
vintage-resolved inventory tractable: the number of vintage layers grows
with the number of intervals, and with it the number of binary activation
variables, so the model size is linear in the horizon while the
branch-and-bound effort is not.

Stored energy at each candidate bus is partitioned into vintages indexed
by $k\in\mathcal{K}=\{0,1,\dots,T\}$. Layer $k=0$ carries the inventory
present at the start of the block, and layer $k\geq1$ is created by
charging in interval $k$.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

\emph{1) Primary economic objective:}
With the planning variables
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}}
=
\{u_i^{\mathrm{es}},P_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}},E_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{es}}}
\label{eq:planning_variables}
\end{equation}
and $\boldsymbol{y}^{\mathrm{op}}$ collecting all scenario-dependent
variables, the primary problem minimizes
$C^{\mathrm{ann}}=C^{\mathrm{inv}}+C^{\mathrm{op}}$, where the annualized
investment is
\begin{equation}
C^{\mathrm{inv}}
=
\mathrm{CRF}
\!\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{es}}}\!\!
\left(
c_i^{\mathrm{site}}u_i^{\mathrm{es}}
+
c_i^P P_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
+
c_i^E E_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
\right)
\label{eq:investment_cost}
\end{equation}
and the annual operating cost is
\begin{equation}
C^{\mathrm{op}}
=c^{\mathrm{dem}}P^{\mathrm{peak}}
+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_s
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
\Gamma_{s,t}\Delta t,
\label{eq:operating_cost}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{s,t}
=\;&c_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}
+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_i}
c_g^{\mathrm{gen}}P_{g,s,t}
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{es}}}c_i^{\mathrm{deg}}
\left(P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}\right)
+c^{\mathrm{delay}}B_{s,t}
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}c^{\mathrm{curt}}P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{curt}}
+c^{\mathrm{VOLL}}P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{shed,DC}}
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}c^{\mathrm{CO_2}}\Xi_{i,s,t}.
\label{eq:interval_cost}
\end{align}
The terms account for grid purchases, local generation, degradation,
workload delay, PV curtailment, data-center load shedding, and carbon
emitted beyond the nodal targets; the demand charge $c^{\mathrm{dem}}$
applies to the annual peak. The last term is developed in
Section~\ref{sec:carbon_target}.

\emph{2) Carbon-vintage preservation objective:}
Let $C^{\mathrm{op},*}$ and $\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl},*}$ denote the
optimal first-stage values. A second stage fixes the planning decisions
and re-optimizes only the operating variables to minimize
\begin{equation}
J^{\mathrm{res}}
=
\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_s
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{es}}}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
\bigl(
\mathscr{w}^{\max}-\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}
\bigr)
P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
\Delta t,
\label{eq:reserve_objective}
\end{equation}
subject to $\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}}=\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl},*}$
and $C^{\mathrm{op}}\leq C^{\mathrm{op},*}+\varepsilon_C^{\mathrm{num}}$.

Because lower-carbon vintages carry larger coefficients in
\eqref{eq:reserve_objective}, minimizing $J^{\mathrm{res}}$ discourages
their unnecessary discharge and so resolves the degeneracy among
economically equivalent dispatches. The tolerance
$\varepsilon_C^{\mathrm{num}}$ absorbs solver precision only and cannot
alter the planning decision. The vintage-specific discharge variables
define a virtual carbon-allocation policy; they do not imply physical
separation of energy inside the storage.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Constraints}
\label{sec:constraints}

Unless stated otherwise the following apply for all $s\in\mathcal{S}$,
$t\in\mathcal{T}$, and all applicable buses, lines, generators, and
vintage layers.

\emph{1) Data-center flexibility:}
Workload arriving at rate $A_{s,t}$ is processed at rate $x_{s,t}$ with
backlog $B_{s,t}$,
\begin{equation}
B_{s,t+1}=B_{s,t}+A_{s,t}-x_{s,t},
\qquad
B_{s,1}=B_{s,T+1}=0,
\label{eq:backlog_dynamic}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
0\leq x_{s,t}\leq\overline{x},
\qquad
0\leq B_{s,t}\leq\overline{B}.
\label{eq:workload_limits}
\end{equation}
Work cannot be processed before it arrives, and must be finished within
$L^{\max}$ intervals of arriving:
\begin{align}
\sum_{t'\leq t}x_{s,t'}
&\leq
\sum_{t'\leq t}A_{s,t'},
\label{eq:no_early_processing}\\
\sum_{t'\leq\min(t+L^{\max},T)}\!\!\!\!x_{s,t'}
&\geq
\sum_{t'\leq t}A_{s,t'}.
\label{eq:deadline_constraint}
\end{align}
The facility power and the resulting nodal load are
\begin{align}
P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{DC}}
&=
P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{aux}}
+\mathrm{PUE}_{s,t}
\bigl(P^{\mathrm{idle}}+\kappa^{\mathrm{task}}x_{s,t}\bigr),
\label{eq:DC_power}\\
P_{i,s,t}^{L}
&=
P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{base}}
+\delta_i^{\mathrm{DC}}
\bigl(P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{DC}}-P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{shed,DC}}\bigr),
\label{eq:nodal_load}
\end{align}
with $\delta_i^{\mathrm{DC}}=1$ at $i=i^{\mathrm{DC}}$ and zero
otherwise, and shedding limited by
$0\leq P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{shed,DC}}\leq
P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{DC}}-P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{DC,crit}}$.

Deferrable workload is the facility's principal flexibility. Because
\eqref{eq:deadline_constraint} permits work to be moved within
$L^{\max}$, the facility can be shifted toward low-carbon intervals
without shedding, which competes with storage as a compliance mechanism
and must therefore be modelled if the storage decision is to be credible.

\emph{2) Shared-storage planning and aggregate operation:}
The planning constraints are
\begin{align}
\underline{P}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_i^{\mathrm{es}}
&\leq P_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
\leq\overline{P}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_i^{\mathrm{es}},
\label{eq:storage_power_capacity}\\
\underline{E}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_i^{\mathrm{es}}
&\leq E_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
\leq\overline{E}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_i^{\mathrm{es}},
\label{eq:storage_energy_capacity}\\
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{es}}}u_i^{\mathrm{es}}
&\leq\overline{N}^{\mathrm{es}},
\label{eq:storage_site_limit}\\
h_i^{\min}P_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
&\leq E_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
\leq h_i^{\max}P_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}.
\label{eq:storage_duration}
\end{align}
Aggregate quantities are sums over layers,
\begin{equation}
P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}
=\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\!P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}},
\quad
P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
=\!\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\!P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}},
\label{eq:aggregate_power}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
e_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}e_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}},
\label{eq:aggregate_energy}
\end{equation}
and obey the usual limits
\begin{align}
0\leq P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}},\,P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
&\leq P_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}},
\label{eq:aggregate_power_limit}\\
u_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}+u_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
&\leq1,
\label{eq:charge_discharge_exclusion}\\
P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}
&\leq\overline{P}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}},
\label{eq:charge_status}\\
P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
&\leq\overline{P}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}},
\label{eq:discharge_status}\\
\underline{\mathrm{SOC}}_iE_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
\leq e_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
&\leq\overline{\mathrm{SOC}}_iE_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}.
\label{eq:SOC_limit}
\end{align}
The layer representation changes the resolution of the carbon accounting
only; it leaves the aggregate physical behaviour of the storage
unaltered.

\emph{3) Charging-time-resolved virtual carbon inventory:}
Layer $k\geq1$ may receive charging energy only in interval $k$, and the
initial layer receives none:
\begin{equation}
P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}=0
\quad\text{for }t\neq k,
\qquad
P_{i,0,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}=0.
\label{eq:layer_charge_time}
\end{equation}
A binary $u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}}$ records whether the layer was
created, gated by a minimum effective charge
$\varepsilon^{\mathrm{ch}}$:
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{\mathrm{ch}}u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}}
\leq P_{i,k,s,k}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}
\leq\overline{P}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}},
\label{eq:layer_activation}
\end{equation}
and a layer can neither discharge before it exists nor hold energy or
carbon unless created:
\begin{align}
P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}&=0,\qquad t<k,
\label{eq:no_discharge_before_formation}\\
0\leq P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
&\leq\overline{P}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}},
\label{eq:layer_discharge_activation}\\
0\leq e_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
&\leq\overline{E}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}},
\label{eq:layer_energy_activation}\\
0\leq m_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
&\leq\mathscr{w}^{\max}\overline{E}_i^{\mathrm{es,cap}}
u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}}.
\label{eq:layer_carbon_activation}
\end{align}
With retention $\kappa_i^{\mathrm{es}}=1-\sigma_i$, the layer energy and
carbon evolve as
\begin{align}
e_{i,k,s,t+1}^{\mathrm{es}}
=\;&\kappa_i^{\mathrm{es}}e_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
+\eta_i^{\mathrm{ch}}P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}\Delta t
\nonumber\\
&-\frac{P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}}{\eta_i^{\mathrm{dc}}}\Delta t,
\label{eq:layer_energy_dynamic}\\
m_{i,k,s,t+1}^{\mathrm{es}}
=\;&\kappa_i^{\mathrm{es}}m_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
+\mathscr{w}_{i,s,t}\eta_i^{\mathrm{ch}}
P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}\Delta t
\nonumber\\
&-\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}
\frac{P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}}{\eta_i^{\mathrm{dc}}}\Delta t,
\label{eq:layer_carbon_dynamic}
\end{align}
subject to
$P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}\Delta t/\eta_i^{\mathrm{dc}}
\leq\kappa_i^{\mathrm{es}}e_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}$ and to the identity
\begin{equation}
m_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}
=\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}e_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es}}.
\label{eq:carbon_energy_relation}
\end{equation}
An active layer inherits the nodal intensity at its formation interval,
imposed conditionally as
\begin{equation}
\bigl|
\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}-\mathscr{w}_{i,s,k}
\bigr|
\leq
M^{\mathrm{big}}\bigl(1-u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}}\bigr),
\label{eq:carbon_intensity_link}
\end{equation}
with $0\leq\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}\leq\mathscr{w}^{\max}
u_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es,lay}}$. Because each layer is charged once, its
intensity is fixed at formation and does not evolve.

Each scenario is initialized independently, with the pre-existing
inventory in layer $0$ and all other layers empty. Since a single block
is modelled, no inter-day transfer is required, and cyclic conditions are
imposed on the aggregates at the block boundaries:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}e_{i,k,s,T+1}^{\mathrm{es}}
=e_{i,0,s,1}^{\mathrm{es}},
\quad
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}m_{i,k,s,T+1}^{\mathrm{es}}
=m_{i,0,s,1}^{\mathrm{es}}.
\label{eq:block_cycle}
\end{equation}
These restore the aggregate energy and carbon inventories without forcing
each individual layer to empty, which would otherwise prohibit exactly
the behaviour the model exists to capture---holding a clean vintage past
the end of the block.

\emph{4) Generation, PV, and grid:}
Generator output and ramping obey
\begin{align}
\underline{P}_{g,s,t}\leq P_{g,s,t}&\leq\overline{P}_{g,s,t},
\qquad
\underline{Q}_{g,s,t}\leq Q_{g,s,t}\leq\overline{Q}_{g,s,t},
\label{eq:generator_limit}\\
\underline{\Delta}_g\leq P_{g,s,t}-P_{g,s,t-1}&\leq\overline{\Delta}_g,
\label{eq:ramp}
\end{align}
with $P_{g,s,0}=P_{g,s}^{\mathrm{ini}}$. PV utilization and curtailment
satisfy
$P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{PV}}+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{curt}}
=\overline{P}_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{PV}}$ with both nonnegative, grid purchase
obeys
$0\leq P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}\leq
a_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}\overline{P}^{\mathrm{grid}}$, and the annual peak
satisfies $P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}\leq P^{\mathrm{peak}}$ for all $s,t$.

\emph{5) LinDistFlow network model:}
Each line is oriented from the substation toward the downstream bus.
Nodal power balance is
\begin{align}
&\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_i}P_{g,s,t}
+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{PV}}
+\delta_{i0}P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}
+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
-P_{i,s,t}^{L}
-P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}
\nonumber\\
&\qquad=
\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}P_{ij,s,t}
-\sum_{h:(h,i)\in\mathcal{L}}P_{hi,s,t},
\label{eq:nodal_active_balance}
\end{align}
with the reactive counterpart, and voltages follow
\cite{Baran1989DistFlow}
\begin{equation}
v_{j,s,t}=v_{i,s,t}-2\bigl(r_{ij}P_{ij,s,t}+x_{ij}Q_{ij,s,t}\bigr),
\label{eq:lindistflow_voltage}
\end{equation}
subject to
$P_{ij,s,t}^{2}+Q_{ij,s,t}^{2}\leq\overline{S}_{ij}^{2}$,
$\underline{V}_i^2\leq v_{i,s,t}\leq\overline{V}_i^2$, and
$v_{0,s,t}=V_0^2$.

Carbon tracing requires the flow direction, so each active line flow is
decomposed into mutually exclusive nonnegative components,
\begin{align}
P_{ij,s,t}&=P_{ij,s,t}^{+}-P_{ij,s,t}^{-},
\label{eq:flow_decomposition}\\
0\leq P_{ij,s,t}^{+}&\leq\overline{S}_{ij}z_{ij,s,t}^{\mathrm{f}},
\label{eq:forward_flow}\\
0\leq P_{ij,s,t}^{-}&\leq\overline{S}_{ij}
\bigl(1-z_{ij,s,t}^{\mathrm{f}}\bigr),
\label{eq:reverse_flow}
\end{align}
with $z_{ij,s,t}^{\mathrm{f}}\in\{0,1\}$. High PV penetration makes
reverse flow routine, and a carbon-flow model that assumed a fixed
direction would misattribute emissions in exactly those intervals where
the intensity is lowest.

\emph{6) Nodal carbon flow and targets:}
Carbon injected by storage discharge carries the intensity of the vintage
that supplies it,
\begin{equation}
R_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}},
\label{eq:storage_carbon_flow}
\end{equation}
which is where this model departs from a well-mixed inventory: the carbon
released depends on \emph{which} stored energy is used, not only on how
much.

Collecting the power and carbon arriving at bus $i$ from every source,
\begin{align}
A_{i,s,t}
=\;&\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_i}P_{g,s,t}
+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{PV}}
+\delta_{i0}P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}
+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
\nonumber\\
&+\!\!\sum_{h:(h,i)\in\mathcal{L}}\!\!P_{hi,s,t}^{+}
+\!\!\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}\!\!P_{ij,s,t}^{-},
\label{eq:incoming_power}\\
B_{i,s,t}
=\;&\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}_i}\mathscr{w}_{g}^{G}P_{g,s,t}
+\delta_{i0}\mathscr{w}_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}P_{s,t}^{\mathrm{grid}}
+R_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
\nonumber\\
&+\!\!\sum_{h:(h,i)\in\mathcal{L}}\!\!\mathscr{w}_{h,s,t}P_{hi,s,t}^{+}
+\!\!\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}\!\!\mathscr{w}_{j,s,t}P_{ij,s,t}^{-},
\label{eq:incoming_carbon}
\end{align}
proportional sharing \cite{chen2025copf} defines the nodal intensity
through the mixing identity
\begin{equation}
\mathscr{w}_{i,s,t}A_{i,s,t}=B_{i,s,t}.
\label{eq:nodal_mixing}
\end{equation}
PV output appears in $A_{i,s,t}$ but not in $B_{i,s,t}$ and so dilutes
the nodal intensity, which is the mechanism by which local generation
relaxes a nodal target.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Carbon Targets and Their Economic Treatment}
\label{sec:carbon_target}

The data-center bus is held to a target $\overline{\mathscr{w}}$ and the
remaining buses to $\overline{\mathscr{w}}^{\mathrm{o}}$. Imposing these
as hard limits is unsatisfactory for a planning study. A target loose
enough to be always attainable does not influence the design at all,
while one tight enough to influence it is, on a system of this size,
already tight enough to be unreachable in some intervals---at which point
the planning problem returns no design rather than an expensive one, and
the sensitivity of siting and sizing to the target cannot be traced.

We therefore admit a nodal excess $\Xi_{i,s,t}\geq0$, in the units of
$B_{i,s,t}$, and price it in \eqref{eq:interval_cost}:
\begin{equation}
\mathscr{w}_{i,s,t}A_{i,s,t}
\leq
\overline{\mathscr{w}}_iA_{i,s,t}+\Xi_{i,s,t},
\label{eq:nodal_carbon_target}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\mathscr{w}}_i=\overline{\mathscr{w}}$ at
$i=i^{\mathrm{DC}}$ and $\overline{\mathscr{w}}^{\mathrm{o}}$ otherwise.

Pricing the excess in the units of the quantity it measures is deliberate,
and is not equivalent to the customary big-$M$ relaxation in which a
dimensionless violation variable is scaled by a bound on nodal power
before entering the constraint. Under that form the implied cost per unit
of carbon is inversely proportional to a numerical bound, so re-deriving
the bound changes the cost of a violation without changing its physical
size, and the value attributed to storage moves with it. Here
$c^{\mathrm{CO_2}}$ is an assumption of the study, stated once and
varied deliberately. The formulation retains both limits:
$c^{\mathrm{CO_2}}\!\to\!\infty$ recovers the hard cap wherever it is
attainable, and $c^{\mathrm{CO_2}}\!=\!0$ recovers carbon-agnostic
planning, so the influence of the target can be traced continuously
between them.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Where the Storage Value Originates}
\label{sec:value_origin}

Substituting \eqref{eq:nodal_mixing} and \eqref{eq:storage_carbon_flow}
into \eqref{eq:nodal_carbon_target} and separating the terms that do not
involve storage discharge isolates what storage contributes to
compliance. Writing $A_{i,s,t}^{\setminus}$ and $B_{i,s,t}^{\setminus}$
for \eqref{eq:incoming_power} and \eqref{eq:incoming_carbon} without
their storage-discharge terms, and
\begin{equation}
D_{i,s,t}
=B_{i,s,t}^{\setminus}-\overline{\mathscr{w}}_iA_{i,s,t}^{\setminus},
\label{eq:carbon_deficit}
\end{equation}
the target becomes
\begin{equation}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
\bigl(\overline{\mathscr{w}}_i-\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}\bigr)
P_{i,k,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,dc}}
+\Xi_{i,s,t}
\geq
D_{i,s,t}.
\label{eq:carbon_gap_priced}
\end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:carbon_gap_priced} states the mechanism the model
exists to represent. A positive $D_{i,s,t}$ is a compliance deficit that
must be met either by discharging a vintage whose intensity lies below
the target---at a rate weighted by how far below it lies---or by paying
for the excess. The planner is therefore rewarded for charging during
low-intensity intervals specifically so that the resulting vintages are
available when $D_{i,s,t}$ is positive, and the worth of an installation
depends on the intensities of the vintages it can hold rather than on its
energy capacity alone.

A well-mixed inventory assigns one intensity to all stored energy and
cannot distinguish a battery charged at the cleanest hours from one
charged at the dirtiest. The coefficient
$\overline{\mathscr{w}}_i-\mathscr{w}_{i,k,s}^{\mathrm{es}}$ is precisely
what that abstraction discards, and \eqref{eq:carbon_gap_priced} shows it
entering the siting and sizing decision directly: where the available
low-carbon layers are insufficient, the first stage must add power or
energy capacity, move the installation, charge more during preceding
low-intensity intervals, or reschedule the deferrable workload.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Tractability of the Nodal Identity}
\label{sec:tractability}

Equations \eqref{eq:nodal_mixing} and \eqref{eq:carbon_energy_relation}
are bilinear, so both stages are mixed-integer nonlinear programs. Two
treatments are available and they are not interchangeable.

Solving the bilinear equalities exactly requires a nonconvex
mixed-integer solver. This is the reference formulation, and we use it to
verify the relaxation below, but its cost grows steeply once a target
binds: on instances where a relaxed solve terminates in under a minute,
the exact solve does not converge within an hour.

The customary alternative replaces each product by its McCormick
envelope. Care is required, because the envelope is useful here in one
direction only. Enforcing \eqref{eq:nodal_carbon_target} requires a lower
bound on $\mathscr{w}_{i,s,t}$, which the envelope supplies as
$\mathscr{w}_{i,s,t}\geq B_{i,s,t}/\overline{A}_i$. That bound weakens in
proportion to the slack in $\overline{A}_i$, and its companion
\begin{equation}
B_{i,s,t}
\geq
\mathscr{w}^{\max}A_{i,s,t}
+\overline{A}_i\mathscr{w}_{i,s,t}
-\mathscr{w}^{\max}\overline{A}_i
\label{eq:mccormick_lower}
\end{equation}
becomes vacuous---dominated by $B_{i,s,t}\geq0$---whenever $A_{i,s,t}$
sits well below $\overline{A}_i$. A bound taken from line thermal
ratings, which on a distribution feeder exceed realized flows by an order
of magnitude, therefore admits solutions in which a line carries no
carbon at all and the nodal target holds at any level.

Bounds must instead be derived from what a bus can absorb. Rearranging
\eqref{eq:nodal_active_balance} and \eqref{eq:flow_decomposition} gives
\begin{equation}
A_{i,s,t}
=P_{i,s,t}^{L}
+P_{i,s,t}^{\mathrm{es,ch}}
+\!\!\sum_{h:(h,i)\in\mathcal{L}}\!\!P_{hi,s,t}^{-}
+\!\!\sum_{j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{L}}\!\!P_{ij,s,t}^{+},
\label{eq:incoming_identity}
\end{equation}
whose terms are nonnegative and individually bounded: by the bus load,
which is data rather than a variable and so exact; by the installed
charging power; and by the demand and the injection of the subtree below
each line. The resulting $\overline{A}_i$ is tighter than a thermal
rating by roughly an order of magnitude, and taking the minimum of the
two can only tighten the relaxation, never admit a solution the rating
excluded.

The tightness of $\overline{A}_i$ should be reported alongside any result
obtained from the relaxed model, and the relaxation checked against the
exact formulation on reduced instances. A carbon target that fails to
alter the objective as it is tightened is not evidence that storage is
unnecessary; it is evidence that the relaxation is not tight enough to
enforce it.

Collecting the above, the primary problem is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}},\boldsymbol{y}^{\mathrm{op}}}
\;
C^{\mathrm{ann}}
\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad
(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}},\boldsymbol{y}^{\mathrm{op}})\in\Omega,
\label{eq:compact_stage1}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is defined by
\eqref{eq:backlog_dynamic}--\eqref{eq:nodal_carbon_target}, and the

\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad

\label{eq:compact_stage2}
\end{equation}
Fixing the operating binaries after the first stage reduces the second to

\subsection{The Selection Problem}
\label{sec:selection_problem}

Let $\xi$ denote an operating scenario---a joint realization over the
48-hour block of load, PV availability, workload arrivals, grid carbon
intensity and prices---distributed as $\mathbb{P}$. Writing
$c(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}},\xi)$ for the optimal operating cost of
\eqref{eq:compact_stage1} under design $\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}}$ and
scenario $\xi$, the planning problem of interest is
\begin{equation}
\min_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}}}
\;
C^{\mathrm{inv}}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}})
+\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathbb{P}}
\bigl[c(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}},\xi)\bigr].
\label{eq:true_problem}
\end{equation}
Each evaluation of $c$ solves a mixed-integer program over the whole
block, so \eqref{eq:true_problem} is solved in practice on a support of
$K$ scenarios $\mathcal{X}=\{\xi_1,\dots,\xi_K\}$ with weights
$\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Delta^{K-1}$:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}(\mathcal{X},\boldsymbol{\pi})
\in
\argmin_{\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}}}
\;
C^{\mathrm{inv}}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}})
+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi_k\,c(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{pl}},\xi_k).
\label{eq:support_problem}
\end{equation}

The support is conventionally chosen to represent $\mathbb{P}$ in
distribution, by clustering or by maximizing coverage. That criterion is
indifferent to what the support is for. Two supports equally
representative in a distributional sense can induce designs of very
different quality, because \eqref{eq:support_problem} responds only to
those features of $\xi$ that bind its constraints---and under
\eqref{eq:carbon_gap_priced} the binding feature is the coincidence of a
compliance deficit with the availability of low-intensity vintages, which
no distributional distance measures. We therefore select the support by
the quality of the decision it induces,
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathcal{X},\boldsymbol{\pi}}
\;
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X},\boldsymbol{\pi})
:=
\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathbb{P}}
\bigl[
c\bigl(\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}(\mathcal{X},\boldsymbol{\pi}),\xi\bigr)
\bigr].
\label{eq:decision_loss}
\end{equation}
The support enters \eqref{eq:decision_loss} only through the design it
produces; a scenario that is unusual but does not change
$\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}$ has no effect on the loss.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Generative Parameterization}
\label{sec:parameterization}

Searching over $\mathcal{X}$ directly is unattractive: scenarios are
high-dimensional and must remain physically admissible, and restricting
the search to observed scenarios reduces \eqref{eq:decision_loss} to a
combinatorial subset selection over the historical record.

We search instead in the latent space of a conditional generator
$G_{\phi}$, trained beforehand on the historical pool, which maps a
latent $\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_z}$ and a context
$\boldsymbol{\omega}$ to a scenario. With
$\boldsymbol{Z}=[\boldsymbol{z}_1,\dots,\boldsymbol{z}_K]$ and
$\boldsymbol{\Omega}=[\boldsymbol{\omega}_1,\dots,\boldsymbol{\omega}_K]$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}(\boldsymbol{Z},\boldsymbol{\Omega})
=\bigl\{G_{\phi}(\boldsymbol{z}_k,\boldsymbol{\omega}_k)\bigr\}_{k=1}^{K},
\label{eq:generated_support}
\end{equation}
and $\boldsymbol{\pi}=\mathrm{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})$. The
generator is held fixed; only $(\boldsymbol{Z},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ are
optimized. The support may therefore leave the historical record while
remaining on the manifold the generator learned, and the search is over
$Kn_z$ continuous variables rather than over subsets.

The contexts $\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ are taken from $K$ observed scenarios
selected by a coverage rule and then held fixed, which anchors each
support member to a distinct region of the conditioning space. This is a
restriction and should be stated as one: any attribute of $\xi$ carried
by the context rather than by the latent cannot be altered by the search,
so the reachable set is a neighbourhood of the anchors rather than the
generator's full range. Whether that neighbourhood contains supports of
the quality the method requires is a property of the generator and the
anchors, not of the optimizer, and is checked directly by comparing the
decision-relevant statistics of generated scenarios---here the within-block
swing of carbon intensity, which \eqref{eq:carbon_gap_priced} shows to be
what storage arbitrages---against those of observed ones.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Score-Function Optimization}
\label{sec:score_function}

The composition in \eqref{eq:decision_loss} is not differentiable in
$(\boldsymbol{Z},\boldsymbol{\lambda})$. The map from support to design is
the $\argmin$ of a mixed-integer program and is piecewise constant:
almost everywhere its gradient vanishes, and where the active set changes
it does not exist. Neither differentiating through the solver nor
smoothing its optimality conditions applies once integer siting variables
are present.

We therefore treat the support as a random variable and optimize the
expected loss. With
\begin{equation}
q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{Z})
=\mathcal{N}\bigl(\boldsymbol{Z};\boldsymbol{\mu},
\sigma^2\boldsymbol{I}\bigr),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\lambda}),
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal{J}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{Z}\sim q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}[\mathcal{L}]$,
the score-function identity gives
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{J}
=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{Z}\sim q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
\bigl[
\mathcal{L}\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{Z})
\bigr],
\label{eq:score_function}
\end{equation}
which requires only evaluations of $\mathcal{L}$, never its derivative.
With $N$ samples per iteration and a baseline $b$ tracking the running
mean of the loss,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{J}}
=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
\bigl(\mathcal{L}^{(n)}-b\bigr)
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
\log q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{Z}^{(n)}).
\label{eq:reinforce}
\end{equation}
The baseline leaves the estimator unbiased and reduces its variance,
which matters because each $\mathcal{L}^{(n)}$ costs one solve of
\eqref{eq:support_problem} followed by a fixed-design evaluation, so $N$
is necessarily small.

The expectation in \eqref{eq:decision_loss} is replaced during training by
an average over a fixed validation set $\mathcal{V}$, held constant across
iterations so that losses from different iterations are comparable, and
disjoint from the test set on which results are reported.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Resolution Limits}
\label{sec:resolution}

Two properties of \eqref{eq:reinforce} determine whether the procedure can
work at all, independently of how it is tuned, and both follow from
$\mathcal{L}$ being the output of a terminated mixed-integer solve.

Each $\mathcal{L}^{(n)}$ is obtained at a relative optimality gap
$\epsilon$. Differences between samples smaller than $\epsilon\mathcal{L}$
are therefore ordered not by the decision but by where the solver stopped,
and if the spread of $\mathcal{L}^{(n)}$ within an iteration is comparable
to $\epsilon\mathcal{L}$, \eqref{eq:reinforce} estimates the gradient of
numerical noise. The gap must be chosen against the spread of the losses
being compared rather than against the magnitude of the objective; the two
differ by orders of magnitude here, because the storage decision moves a
small fraction of total system cost while every design pays the same
untouchable remainder.

The same threshold governs convergence. An update changing the loss by
less than $\epsilon\mathcal{L}$ is indistinguishable from no update, so a
run whose total improvement falls below that threshold has not been shown
to have learned anything, however smooth its trajectory appears. We
therefore report improvement relative to $\epsilon\mathcal{L}$ rather than
in absolute terms, and report the displacement
$\|\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_0\|$ of the policy mean alongside it:
a run in which the loss did not move while the mean did was searching,
whereas one whose mean travelled less than the width $\sigma$ it samples
at never left its starting neighbourhood.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Selection Among Candidates}
\label{sec:finalists}

A stochastic policy visits many supports and its last iterate need not be
its best. We retain the $F$ distinct designs of lowest validation loss
encountered during training, together with the design induced by the
deterministic support $\boldsymbol{\mu}$, and select among them on
$\mathcal{V}$. Candidates differing by less than $\epsilon\mathcal{L}$ are
treated as tied, and ties are broken toward the smaller installation,
which avoids reporting capacity the evaluation cannot show to be
worthwhile.

This step is a selection over sampled candidates, not a consequence of the
gradient, and it accounts for part of any advantage over choosing a
support at random. Separating the two contributions requires a control
that draws the same number of candidates from $q_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$
with $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ held at its initial value and applies the same
selection: what that control does not achieve is what the policy update
contributed.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

All methods are compared by the value of the design each produces, on one
test subset $\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{te}}$ and against one reference:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{m}}
=
\Bigl[
\min_{\boldsymbol{y}^{\mathrm{op}}}
\!\!\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{te}}}\!\!c(\boldsymbol{0},\xi)
\Bigr]
-
\Bigl[
C^{\mathrm{inv}}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}_{\mathrm{m}})
+\!\!\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{te}}}\!\!
c(\boldsymbol{x}^{\star}_{\mathrm{m}},\xi)
\Bigr].
\label{eq:storage_value}
\end{equation}
Total system cost is a poor denominator, since the storage decision moves
a few percent of it and every design pays the same remainder;
$V_{\mathrm{m}}$ measures what installing storage is worth, net of its own
investment. The first term is common to all methods, so ranking by
$V_{\mathrm{m}}$ and by out-of-sample cost is identical---and a difference
in $V$ is meaningful only when both designs were evaluated against the
same reference on the same subset, which we verify rather than assume.

Two quantities bound the comparison. The perfect-information value
$V^{\mathrm{PI}}$, obtained by optimizing the design directly on
$\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{te}}$, is attainable by no selection rule and
converts values into captured fractions; where that solve terminates early
we report the interval implied by its incumbent and dual bound rather than
a point estimate. And because a stochastic method must be compared against
a distribution rather than a draw, we report the spread of $V$ over
repeated runs alongside its mean, and take as the reference point the
strongest rule requiring neither learning nor a favourable draw.
